# DesktopTools 1.3.0

Recordings of ordinary desktop work no longer freeze or lose their ending, screenshots can be copied and saved in one step, and the interface has icons and motion where it used to be plain text. Optional local models add a second text reader, recording transcripts, higher-quality background removal and face suggestions. Smart screenshot names and hidden file details have their own controls.

## Screen recording

On a screen that was not changing, the recorder wrote one frame a second, periodically threw away frames still waiting for the encoder, and dropped whatever followed the last frame when you stopped. A recording of a document or a tutorial played back with freezes and a missing ending.

- An unchanged screen now repeats its last frame every 100 ms, and queued frames are no longer discarded.
- Measured on a 1920×1080 display at a 144 FPS target for 15 seconds: with the screen repainting every 4 seconds, the longest gap between frames fell from 2353 ms to 111 ms and the file kept 14.5 s instead of 12.8 s. Continuous motion was already smooth and is unchanged.
- The recorder no longer reports the file's frame rate after saving. A still screen averages about 10 FPS by design, and the number read as a fault.
- The audio meters stop querying devices while both audio sources are off.

## Screenshots

- **Clipboard and folder.** Settings → Capture → After capture has a third choice that copies the screenshot and writes a PNG to the save location without a dialog. Until you choose a folder it uses `Pictures\DesktopTools`. Files are never overwritten, and the notice offers **Show in folder**.
- **Region selection.** A loupe shows the exact pixels under the pointer with their coordinates, the selection shows its size in pixels, and the selection has corner markers. With Freeze screen on, moving the mouse repaints much less of the screen.
- **Editor.** Extract text is a labelled button in the toolbar beside Check screenshot instead of an entry in the More menu. Tools have keys (V, P, H, A, R, O, T, B, E, C), Delete removes the selected annotation, Ctrl+C copies, Ctrl+S saves and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes. There are eight colour swatches and the one in use is ringed.
- **Zoom in the editor.** The mouse wheel zooms at the pointer (up to 1600%), Shift+wheel moves sideways, Ctrl+wheel moves up and down, Alt+wheel changes the stroke thickness, and dragging with the middle button moves around. Ctrl+0 or the zoom badge fits the picture again; Ctrl+plus and Ctrl+minus zoom at the centre. From 200% the picture shows its real pixels instead of a smoothed preview. Drawing works at any zoom.

## Interface

- Every settings row has an icon, and plain text buttons and menu entries get one that matches their wording, in every language.
- Windows rise into place when they open; menus and drop-down lists unfold from their anchor. All of it follows the Animations setting and the Windows animation preference.
- A row's icon lights up while the pointer is on it.
- DesktopTools has its own app icon in place of the borrowed Fluent grid icon, and the default accent colour for new settings is purple (#6A45E0) instead of blue. An accent colour already saved in your settings is kept; choose it again under Appearance to switch.
- A **Support** button in the sidebar and a support card in About open the optional support page. Every feature stays free; support is voluntary.
- The sidebar is shorter: **Diagnostics** is a tab in Settings, and **Help** opens from **About → Help and learning**.
- The installer is redesigned in the new purple brand: a side rail with the app icon and a step tracker, calmer cards, a status line that stays in place with a progress bar under it, and branded check boxes and choices.

## Website

The project website is rebuilt around real app screenshots, available in English, German, Spanish, French and Russian, and mentions the optional AI models. A new support page lists ways to help, including wallet addresses with copy buttons, QR codes and explorer links.

## Languages

Text that stayed English in other languages is translated: the window pin notices, shortcut conflict messages, a few editor labels, and the language names in the translation picker. Built-in profile names, product names and the diagnostic report are intentionally unchanged.

## Model packs and licence consent

**Settings → AI models** lists optional packs, their size, licence and state. Before a download, a consent window shows the publisher, source, revision, full licence, training-data disclosure and limitations. Download stays disabled until you accept the licence. Files come from the publisher's URL and must match the built-in size and SHA-256 checksums. Failed or cancelled downloads do not install a partial pack. A download shows megabytes done of the total on the page and in a notification with its own **Cancel** button, so it can be followed from anywhere; the notification says when the model is ready. The **Source** link in the consent window opens the publisher's page in your browser.

**AI models folder** on the same page chooses where packs are kept, for example on a larger drive. DesktopTools creates a `DesktopTools Models` folder inside the folder you pick, copies the installed packs and licence acceptances there, and removes the old folder only after everything is copied. **Use default** moves them back. Translation packs stay in their own folder. Uninstall and the full-data reset remove the default folder only; a folder you chose elsewhere stays until you delete it.

Images, recordings and text are never uploaded. The download host can see your IP address. Packs and acceptance records stay on this PC, survive updates and repairs, and can be removed from the AI models page or by an explicit full-data reset. New translation-pack downloads use the same consent window; previously downloaded packs keep working. The bundled background model and English–Russian translation still work without an optional download.

## OCR pack

**Text tools → Text from screenshot → Text reader** offers **DesktopTools OCR pack** alongside **Windows OCR**, which remains the default. The optional PP-OCRv5 models need a 4.8 MB text finder plus a Latin (8.0 MB), East Slavic Cyrillic (7.9 MB) or Chinese/Japanese (16.7 MB including its character list) recognizer. Scan screen text, Extract text and the screenshot library follow this choice. A missing or damaged pack falls back to Windows OCR with a notice, when a Windows recognizer is available. Extract text in the screenshot and image editors offers **Better text detection** when the pack is not downloaded (licence first, with download progress), and **Use the OCR pack** when it is downloaded but Windows OCR is still chosen.

In the recorded benchmark of 120 generated images with 788 English and Russian phrases (seed 7), the pack read 99.2% of phrases exactly, against 92.6% with the existing Windows OCR preparation. It took 48 seconds including loading, against 33 seconds. This measures generated text, not accuracy on every screenshot. Hardware and further results: [Optional AI models](../ai-models.md#ocr-pack).

## Transcripts

The video editor has a **Transcript** tab. Choose **Fast** (Whisper base, about 292 MB) or **Accurate** (Whisper small, about 969 MB), select the spoken language or Automatic, then **Transcribe**. The recorder's saved-recording notice also offers Transcribe when the video editor is enabled. First use shows a notice about consent when recording or transcribing other people.

- Search the timed lines, choose a time to seek the video, and correct the text. Corrections stay in app data with that video's transcript.
- Transcription writes an SRT beside the video. An existing caption file that DesktopTools did not write is replaced only after confirmation. **Save captions** also offers VTT and plain text.
- After an MP4 export, the editor offers captions retimed to the trim and removed section. Captions are separate files; there are no burned-in captions, speaker labels or live transcription.

Feasibility runs on the development PC took 2.9–3.6 seconds per audio minute with Fast and 8.0–10.5 seconds with Accurate. On two 60-second public-domain English/Russian readings, word error rates were 8.5%/18.8% for Fast and 7.1%/11.1% for Accurate. These are small samples. Speech models can mishear words or invent text during silence, music and noise; check their output.

## Smart file names and Remove hidden details

**Settings → Capture → File names → Smart** suggests names from the captured app and window title using fixed rules, with a date and time. Clipboard and folder auto-save can also try one Windows OCR headline when the title is absent or generic, with an 800 ms budget. The Save dialog does not wait for OCR. Date and time remains the default, and existing files are never overwritten. Parts that look sensitive are dropped, but window titles can still reveal document names or message subjects: review names before sharing.

**Remove hidden details** is an unticked option in Hide private data and the Image tools export dialog. It lists detected categories such as location, device, date and author and removes embedded metadata from the exported copy. The source is unchanged. The choice resets for each opened image. It does not hide visible content, rename the file or clean recordings, GIF or WebP exports. Some export paths already discard metadata even when unticked.

## Background quality pack

**Image tools → Background → Background model** adds **High quality (optional download)**, using BiRefNet-lite (224 MB). Built-in remains available. Choosing High quality asks for the licence before downloading; a missing or damaged pack is reported instead of silently using Built-in.

On generated composites, partly transparent edge error fell from 0.41 with Built-in to 0.22 with the quality model. It improved hair against busy backgrounds but was worse on some glass and plain-background cases. On the development PC, a first in-app removal took 14.6 seconds including loading and the next took 8.6 seconds. The model needed about 5.7 GB of private memory; the app refuses it when less than 6 GiB of commit memory is available and suggests Built-in.

After background removal, **Touch up → Restore / Erase** paints on the mask with a 4–200 px brush. Each stroke is one undo step, and export uses the corrected mask. Other edits or opening another image end Touch up.

## Face suggestions

**Check screenshot** in the screenshot editor offers an optional face finder (YuNet, 229 KB). It suggests areas to cover; nothing is hidden until you accept a suggestion. It locates faces without identifying people or saving face templates. Text checks still run if the face model is missing, damaged or busy.

On one public-domain astronaut photo, a 60 px face was found in 156 ms including loading and a 200 px face in 38 ms. Five generated faceless screenshots stayed below the detection threshold. This does not establish accuracy on crowds or a wide range of faces.

## Translation improvements

Text tools accepts up to **20,000 characters**, retains paragraph separators and shows progress for longer passages. Beam search considers several possible translations per sentence part. On 40 own-written sentence pairs each way, chrF rose from 69.7 to 70.2 for English→Russian and from 72.9 to 74.0 for Russian→English, taking about 1.45 times as long as the earlier search. A 20,000-character passage took about 3.6 minutes on the development PC. This is a small benchmark, not a guarantee of translation quality. Names, numbers and technical terms still need review.

## Under the hood

- ONNX Runtime 1.31.1, Microsoft.ML.Tokenizers 2.0.1, QRCoder 1.8.1 and SkiaSharp 4.155.0. No known vulnerable packages before or after.
- The recorder backend is built from two patches in `patches/`; see `docs/screen-recorder.md`.
- `AiRuntime` serializes local inference and keeps one set of sessions loaded at a time, including both OCR models or all translation stages. Idle sessions are released after about a minute; switching sets releases the previous one. Quit requests release too.
- Speech audio is decoded locally through Media Foundation in 30-second windows. Quiet windows are skipped, and unused encoder attention outputs are removed in memory to reduce memory use. Downloaded model files are unchanged.
- The quality background model disables ONNX Runtime's memory arena to release working memory after a run.
- Image export prepares decoded images before worker-thread encoding and no longer reapplies their original colour profile to pixels already converted to sRGB.
- Model provenance, licences and checksums are in [the model inventory](../licenses/optional-models.md).

## Known limits

- **Not tested:** recording games or full-screen exclusive content with the new timing, recordings longer than 15 seconds with it, the memory plateau runs from 1.2.9 with the new backend, graphics cards other than the NVIDIA card on the development PC, mixed-DPI and HDR displays, and Windows ARM64.
- The loupe, the editor keys and the new animations were checked in rendered images and automated checks, not by hand with a mouse and keyboard on every display setup.
- The Ukrainian, Chinese, Portuguese, Japanese, Polish, Turkish and Italian texts were written without a native-speaker review; corrections are welcome.
- **Not tested:** broader real Chinese/Japanese OCR accuracy (only one short line of each was smoke-tested); speech accuracy outside English and Russian; microphone transcription; recordings of 30 minutes or more and their memory/cancellation behavior; many-speaker, noisy or music-only recordings beyond the small fixtures.
- **Not tested:** background quality on PCs with less than 16 GB RAM; face accuracy on crowds, small faces, different skin tones and ages beyond the single photo; broader translation quality and performance outside the measured pairs.
- **Not tested:** physical mixed-DPI and HDR interaction, remote-viewer visibility, other Windows builds, AMD/Intel or integrated-only PC configurations, ARM64 execution, and interactive update/repair/uninstall with installed packs in this documentation pass. Automated rendering and API checks do not establish sharing behavior.
- **Not tested:** the design's 200-screenshot / 50-editor memory plateau run with this update. Session idle release does not establish that ordinary-use memory growth is fixed.
- **Not tested:** native-speaker review of the new consent and feature wording in all 11 translated catalogs. The AI wording review is recorded in [the language review](../localization-review-1.3.0.md); no native speaker reviewed it.
- OCR can miss small, vertical or strongly rotated text. Cyrillic here means Russian, Ukrainian and Belarusian; it does not cover every Cyrillic language. Sensitive-data and face suggestions can miss content or flag ordinary content.
- There is no AI graphics-card support in this release. Upscaling and GPU support are planned for a later update. The object eraser and ISNet are not included.

Measurements above were recorded during feature work on an Intel Core i5-11600K (6 cores, 12 threads), 16 GB RAM, Windows 11 Pro build 26300.9711, .NET SDK 10.0.402 and ONNX Runtime 1.31.1, processor only. They are single-PC observations, not performance guarantees, and were not rerun for this documentation pass.
